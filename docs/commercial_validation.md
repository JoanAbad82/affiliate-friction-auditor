# Commercial Validation - Phase 1.5

## Current Status

Affiliate Friction Auditor is a private/internal audit toolkit. It is not open source, not a public download, and not a finished SaaS.

Public positioning: `In preparation - Private audits available`.

Current business model: productized service. We run the tool internally, manually review the results, and deliver an audit package to the client.

## What Is Being Sold

Affiliate friction optimization for commercial hubs and listing pages.

The sold offer is a private audit package:

- Review selected commercial hubs/listings.
- Check whether destination posts already contain monetized links or clear commercial intent.
- Identify hub/listing CTA friction.
- Deliver a prioritized opportunity matrix.
- Deliver affiliate friction findings, suggested CTAs, and implementation backlog.
- Include manual review before recommendations are delivered.

## What Is Not Being Sold

- Not generic SEO.
- Not link building.
- Not banner stuffing.
- Not public SaaS access.
- Not a downloadable tool.
- Not source code access.
- Not a full-site crawl by default.
- Not a promise of revenue, ranking, traffic, or merchant approval.

## Why It Should Not Be Downloadable Yet

The toolkit should remain internal during validation because:

- The workflow still needs manual review to avoid overstating findings.
- Site-specific configuration and edge cases are still being refined.
- Public access would create support, onboarding, and expectation-management burdens too early.
- The value proposition is the audited recommendation package, not tool access.
- Keeping delivery manual helps validate whether clients value the outcome before building product infrastructure.

## Validation Objective

Validate whether affiliate publishers, niche site owners, and agencies will pay for a narrow private audit focused on affiliate friction in commercial hubs/listing pages.

The first validation target is to sell or strongly qualify demand for the Starter offer:

- Up to 3 hubs/listings.
- Up to 30 cards/destination posts.
- Opportunity matrix.
- Manual review.
- Prioritized implementation backlog.
- Short executive summary.

## Success Criteria

After the first 10 relevant conversations:

- At least 2 prospects request a concrete follow-up using their URLs.
- At least 1 prospect agrees to pay for a Starter audit or gives a clear buying condition.
- Prospects understand the positioning without reframing it as generic SEO.
- The 250-400 EUR validation price does not create immediate resistance from qualified prospects.
- The required inputs are realistic for prospects to provide.
- The deliverable is seen as actionable by editorial, SEO, affiliate, or development teams.

## 10-Contact Validation Plan

1. Identify 10 relevant contacts from the target segments in `sales/target_contacts.md`.
2. Prioritize contacts with visible commercial hubs, deal pages, comparison pages, or affiliate disclosures.
3. Send a short, direct outreach message.
4. Ask whether they want a small review of 2-3 hubs/listings.
5. Do not ask for broad site access in the first message.
6. If they respond, qualify the site shape and decision process.
7. Offer the Starter audit only when there is a fit.
8. Track objections in a simple notes file or CRM.
9. Track whether prospects ask for examples, pricing, implementation, or reporting.
10. After 10 conversations, decide whether to continue, reposition, narrow, or pause.

## What To Measure

- Reply rate.
- Positive reply rate.
- Number of prospects willing to share 2-3 URLs.
- Number of discovery calls booked.
- Number of Starter audit offers sent.
- Number of paid audits closed.
- Main objections.
- Confusing phrases in the positioning.
- Whether prospects see the audit as SEO, CRO, affiliate operations, or technical review.
- Whether price resistance appears before or after scope is explained.
- Estimated manual effort per audit.
- Quality and actionability of the resulting backlog.

## Decision Tree After First 10 Conversations

If 2 or more prospects are ready to pay:

- Deliver the Starter audits manually.
- Document effort, delivery time, objections, and repeatable findings.
- Keep the tool private.
- Refine the one-pager and outreach based on buyer language.

If prospects are interested but not ready to pay:

- Offer one tightly scoped paid pilot at the lower end of the validation price.
- Ask what proof, sample, or stakeholder approval is missing.
- Improve the one-pager and example deliverable structure.

If prospects ask for implementation support:

- Keep the audit as the entry offer.
- Add optional implementation support as an upsell.
- Avoid bundling implementation into the Starter scope too early.

If prospects think it is generic SEO:

- Tighten positioning around commercial hub/listing friction.
- Lead with the hub -> destination post -> merchant CTA path.
- Avoid broad language like "site audit" in outreach.

If prospects mainly want a tool or SaaS:

- Do not open public access yet.
- Ask what workflow they expected and why.
- Consider a future managed beta only after paid audit demand is clearer.

If there is little interest:

- Revisit the target segment.
- Test agencies and portfolio operators before abandoning the offer.
- Consider narrowing to deal/coupon/comparison pages where the pain is more visible.

## Risks And Constraints

- The audit can be misunderstood as generic SEO unless the positioning stays narrow.
- Automated findings can be wrong without manual review.
- Some sites may not allow meaningful crawling or review.
- Affiliate tracking and merchant rules vary by program.
- Revenue impact depends on traffic, user intent, implementation quality, tracking, and merchant conversion.
- The service should not imply guaranteed outcomes.
- Private client URLs, screenshots, and outputs must remain private unless explicitly approved.
- Public materials must not link to the private repository or imply the tool is downloadable.
